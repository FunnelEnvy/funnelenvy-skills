# Acme Competitive Analysis

## Market Overview

Mid-market distributors choose between ERP add-on modules and enterprise supply chain suites. Both major competitors claim fewer stockouts.

## Competitors

| Name | Size | Positioning |
|---|---|---|
| Northwind | Major | Forecasting module sold as an add-on to its own ERP |
| Globex | Major | Enterprise supply chain suite moving down-market |

### Northwind

- **Positioning:** "Forecasting built into the ERP you already run." Homepage hero claims fewer stockouts.
- **What's actually true:** Customers on its ERP get forecasting with no new vendor. Reviews praise the single login.
- **Where they're weak:** Generic models with no warehouse-level tuning; forecasting works only on its own ERP.
- **Where Acme wins:** Warehouse-level forecasts and a buyer approval queue; works across three ERP systems.
- **Where Acme loses:** A Northwind ERP customer pays nothing extra for its module.
- **Strategic signals:** Sales pitch says a separate forecasting vendor "adds integration risk".
- **Key stats:** Founded 2004; privately held; about 900 employees; 4.1 of 5 across 210 reviews; module bundled with ERP license.

### Globex

- **Positioning:** "End-to-end supply chain planning." Claims fewer stockouts at enterprise scale.
- **What's actually true:** Deep features and strong analyst coverage for distributors over 1,000 employees.
- **Where they're weak:** Six-to-nine-month implementations; pricing starts well above mid-market budgets.
- **Where Acme wins:** Live in 14 days against months; per-warehouse pricing a mid-market budget can carry.
- **Where Acme loses:** Distributors planning to grow past 20 warehouses, or needing transport and supplier planning in one suite.
- **Strategic signals:** No competitive sales intelligence on Globex's objections to Acme is on file.
- **Key stats:** Founded 1998; public; about 4,000 employees; 4.3 of 5 across 640 reviews; custom enterprise pricing.

## Claim Overlap

- "Fewer stockouts": claimed by Acme, Northwind and Globex.

## White Space

- Speed to value for the mid-market. No competitor claims a time-to-first-forecast.
- Buyer-first workflow. Both competitors pitch planners and analysts.
