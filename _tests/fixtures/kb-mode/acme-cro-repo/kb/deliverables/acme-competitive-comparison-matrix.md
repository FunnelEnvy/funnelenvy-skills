# Competitive Comparison: Acme vs. Market

## How to Read This Matrix

Strong means claimed and proven. Moderate means claimed with thin proof, Weak means absent or contradicted, and N/A means the dimension does not apply.

## Comparison Grid

| Dimension | Acme | Northwind | Globex |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary Audience** | Mid-market distributors | Its own ERP customers | Distributors over 1,000 employees |
| **Core Positioning** | Fast forecasting for buyers | Forecasting inside the ERP | End-to-end supply chain planning |
| **Speed to Value** | Strong | Moderate | Weak |
| **Fewer Stockouts** | Strong | Moderate | Moderate |
| **Proof Strength** | 3/5 | 2/5 | 4/5 |
| **Pricing Model** | Per warehouse | Bundled with ERP | Custom enterprise |

## Key Findings

### Where Acme Wins

- Live in 14 days against months for Globex.
- Warehouse-level forecasts that Northwind's generic module lacks.

### Where Acme Loses

- Northwind's module costs its ERP customers nothing extra.

### White Space

- No competitor claims a time to first forecast.

### Claim Overlap (Danger Zone)

- All three claim fewer stockouts.

## Competitor Snapshots

Northwind sells forecasting as an add-on to its own ERP; its threat is the free bundle. Globex is an enterprise suite moving down-market; its threat is analyst coverage.
